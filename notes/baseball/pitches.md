# Pitch Notes

Quick notes from a YouTube breakdown of pitch types.

## Fastballs

Cutter, four-seam, sinker.

### Four-seam fastball

- Goal: overwhelming power.
- Higher arm slot + higher RPM + better spin direction → more induced vertical break (IVB).
- Fights gravity more effectively, so it drops less than hitters expect.
- Batters can swing underneath it.

### Cutter

- Slower alternative to the four-seam.
- Rotated four-seam spin.
- Wants a slight amount of movement; too much movement can hurt the pitch.

### Sinker

- Two types: true sinker and seam-shifted wake sinker.
- True sinker relies on pronation for arm-side run.
- Seam-shifted sinker can create run without relying as heavily on pronation.
- Velocity is similar to a four-seam.
- Better for ground balls than whiffs.

## Breaking balls

Curveball, slider family.

- Movement primarily comes from the Magnus effect.
- Don't need to be completely unrecognizable out of the hand.
- Deception comes from how the pitch breaks relative to the fastball.

### Curveball

- Typically slower than other breaking balls.
- Supination + grip create topspin.
- Primarily used for vertical break.

### Slider family

Main split: **gyroslider vs. sweeper**.

The big difference is spin efficiency.

#### Gyroslider

- Very low/zero spin efficiency.
- Gyro spin contributes little to movement.
- Football-like rotation.
- Can disguise well with fastballs.
- More downward movement.

#### Sweeper

- High glove-side movement.
- Supination + sidespin.
- More lateral break.

## Offspeed

Splitter, changeup, forkball.

- Generally designed around deception and/or speed separation.
- Similar overall concept, but different grips and movement profiles.

### Splitter

- Little obvious change in appearance out of the hand.
- Sharp downward movement with some arm-side break.
- More fingers on the ball at release → more friction → lower spin rate.
- Effective against flatter swings.
- Less effective against hitters with swing paths that handle the drop well.

### Changeup

- Slower pitch with arm-side movement.
- Useful mental model: a slower sinker with lower velocity and spin.

### Forkball

- Similar to a splitter but with a much wider grip.
- Lower velocity and spin → more drop.
- Easier to recognize out of the hand because of the slower velocity.
- Harder to command and disguise.
- Trades some tunneling for more movement.

## Knuckleball

Specialists only.

- Movement comes from the seams affecting airflow around the ball.
- If it doesn't move, you basically have a ~75 mph fastball.
